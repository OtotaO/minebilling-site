E/M visit-mix self-check

Enter how many office visits you billed at each level and see your own percentage mix as a bar chart — plus, if you enter a second period, how the mix moved. Everything runs in your browser; no counts leave your device and no patient data is involved. This tool describes your distribution. It does not tell you what your distribution should be, and it never puts a dollar figure on a difference.

The level of a visit is determined by the documentation — total time on the date of the encounter, or medical decision making — not by where a clinic sits in a distribution. For the actual rules and thresholds, use the 2021 E/M office-visit level calculator.

    Limits of this comparison — our wording, not CMS text. CMS publishes the counts; it does not publish an interpretation, so everything below is ours. Read it before using the comparison for anything:

        What this tool deliberately does not do

        Search results for “E/M visit mix” are dominated by material whose implicit message is that a mix weighted toward higher levels is a better mix. We won't say that, because it isn't a coding rule — it's a sales argument.

        • No target distribution, no “ideal” curve, and no scoring of your mix.
        • No dollar amount attached to any difference. Converting a percentage gap into “money you're leaving on the table” assumes the higher level was documented, which this tool has no way to know and neither does anyone else selling you that number.
        • No external comparison at all unless you ask for one, and when you do, its year, payer population, specialty and published caveats appear next to it.
        • A clinic that codes conservatively when documentation is ambiguous will tend to sit below a national curve. That is a defensible place to be, not a defect to fix.

        What is informative without asserting any outside authority is your own trend: the same clinic, two periods. Comparing yourself to yourself removes the payer-mix, billing-specialty and single-year problems entirely, because both numbers come from the same place — which is why it's the default here. It is not a complete answer either: a mix can move because the panel changed, because a provider joined or left, or because a different set of patients came through the door. A shift tells you where to look; it does not tell you why.

        Want someone to look at the documentation behind the mix?

        Sources and method

        Percentages are computed in your browser as each code's count divided by the total for that code family, so shares within a family sum to 100% (displayed values are rounded to one decimal and may not add to exactly 100). Shifts are simple differences in percentage points between the two periods you entered. The optional reference distribution is CMS, Medicare Part B Physician/Supplier National Data — Evaluation and Management Codes by Specialty (CY2024 file), computed as each code's ALLOWED SERVICES divided by the family total; cells CMS suppresses (fewer than 11 allowed services) are omitted, never estimated. 99201 was deleted effective 2021-01-01 (CMS Pub 100-04 Transmittal 10505, CR 12071), so the new-patient family is 99202–99205.
